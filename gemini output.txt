   1. Responsive Web Design (RWD) Fundamentals:

       * Flexible Layouts: Instead of fixed pixel widths, they use

       * Viewport Meta Tag: A crucial line in the HTML <head> (<meta

         tells the mobile browser to render the page at its actual device 
         width, not a zoomed-out desktop version. Without this, nothing   
         else works correctly.

   2. Mobile-First Approach:
       * Instead of designing for desktop and then shrinking, many modern 
         sites design for the smallest screen first. This forces them to  
         prioritize content and functionality, leading to a cleaner, more 
         focused mobile experience. Then, they progressively enhance the  
         design for larger screens.

   3. User Experience (UX) Considerations:
       * Touch Targets: Buttons and interactive elements are made larger  
         and have enough spacing around them to be easily tappable with a 
         finger.
       * Simplified Navigation: Menus are often condensed into "hamburger"
         icons or bottom navigation bars.
       * Optimized Content: Text sizes, line heights, and image resolutios
          are adjusted for readability and faster loading on mobile.

       * This is often what gives sites like Flipkart that truly native ap
          feel. PWAs are regular websites that use modern web capabilities
         to deliver an app-like experience. This includes:
           * Offline Access: They can work even without an internet

           * "Add to Home Screen": Users can "install" them directly from 
             the browser to their device's home screen, launching them

             apps.
