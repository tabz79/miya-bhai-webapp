   * Backend (your `src/server.js`): This is your restaurant's

  Right now, you have a beautiful dining area (your live website
  on Cloudflare), but the kitchen is still running on your

  the kitchen needs to be in the same building (i.e., also on the
  internet).

  Can I deploy my backend to Cloudflare Pages?

  No. Cloudflare Pages is designed for "static" sites (the frontend       
   user interface). It can't run a Node.js server like your
  src/server.js. You need a different kind of hosting service for
  your backend.

  Where can I deploy my backend?

  There are many services designed specifically for this. They are        
  made to be very simple to use. Here are a few popular options
  that have free tiers to get you started:

   * Render: Very popular and easy to use for Node.js apps. You can       
     often connect it directly to your GitHub repository.
   * Fly.io: Another great option that is known for good
     performance.
   * Heroku: A classic platform for deploying apps, also very
     straightforward.

  What's the final step?

  Once you deploy your backend to a service like Render, you will
  get a public URL for it, for example:
  https://miya-bhai-backend.onrender.com.

  The final step is to tell your frontend application to use this
  new URL. Instead of using the proxy to localhost:3000, you will
  configure your frontend code to make requests directly to your
  new public backend URL. This is typically done with an
  environment variable in your Cloudflare Pages settings.

  So, the production workflow looks like this:

   1. A user visits https://miya-bhai-webapp-v1.pages.dev.
   2. The frontend app loads in their browser.
   3. When the app needs to fetch the menu, it makes a request to
      https://miya-bhai-backend.onrender.com/api/menu.
   4. Your deployed backend receives the request, gets the data from      
      your Supabase database, and sends it back to the frontend.

  Your local development setup with npm run dev will remain the
  same for when you are building and testing new features. The
  deployment step is what makes it available to the world.
